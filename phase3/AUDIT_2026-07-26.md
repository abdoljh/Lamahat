## Note 2 — "sentence cut across shots"  → CONFIRMED, worse than reported

Boundary classification of the 87 interior cuts against the narrated
punctuation stream:

| kind | count |
|---|---|
| on a sentence end | 11 |
| on a clause end (، ؛) | 3 |
| **mid-sentence** | **73 (84 %)** |

The P7.2 post-hoc snapper (±0.9 s) rescues only 12 of the 73 — this plan's
cuts are too far from sentence ends to nudge.  **The prompt fix is the real
fix; a re-PLAN is required, not a re-render.**  The sentence caption track
built from this narration: 80 events, avg 5.2 s, 60 of which span at least
one cut (i.e. 60 subtitles would previously have been chopped).

## Note 3 — era restriction  → CONFIRMED, quantified

44 of 54 image shots (81 %) carry a period era; only 10 are timeless.  The
44 period shots use **37 distinct era strings** ("1900s Ottoman Empire
multi-ethnic officer corps", "1910s Ottoman Empire political decay", …) —
each one a separate archival-search demand, guaranteeing starvation under
the P6 hard gate.  P7.3 softens the gate (era-0-only rejection, Pexels as
era-gated last resort) and steers thematic beats to "timeless".

## NEW finding — script/narration divergence (word 407)

The narration (and word_timings.json) says
«أم ينضم إلى صفوف الثورة العربية ضد الحكم العثماني؟» where every stored
`main_script.txt` says «أم ينحاز إلى شعبه العربي الذي يحلم بالحرية؟».
One revised sentence → every later word is offset by one token (652 vs
651).  The rendered cut is safe (plan quotes match the audio), but any
future align/plan run mixing the revised script with the old narration will
misalign the last third of the film.  **Rule: after any script edit, either
re-synthesize the narration or revert the script — never mix.**
P7.2's length-mismatch guard degrades gracefully here (falls back to
token-tail punctuation), by design.

   to the narrated sentence).
2. Run the **total solution** on `claude/phase3-movie-quality-d1n58l` —

   note 2 (84 % mid-sentence cuts are baked into that plan).

   — the Sonnet bank assignment matches by CONTENT, not shot number, so
   the curation survives replanning (shot numbers will change).

   since this cut was rendered with captions off.
